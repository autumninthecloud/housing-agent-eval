# Phase 1 Narrative: Class C Violation Concentration by Zip Code

The single highest Class C concentration in `top10.csv` is zip **02018** at 100% (1 of 1 total violations). This row is not a meaningful signal: it reflects a single record, and the analyst flagged it as a likely corrupted/artifact postcode (not a real NYC zip) rather than a genuine concentration finding. The visualizer's charts (`charts/stacked_bar.png`, `charts/trend_line.png`) label it as a single-record artifact rather than excluding it.

Setting that row aside, the next-highest percentage is **10006** at 60.0%, but on a small base (90 total violations). The more actionable concentration signal comes from zips that combine a high Class C share with substantial violation volume: **10475** (39.72% of 1,080 total), **10010** (35.6% of 1,000), **10009** (35.52% of 4,231), **10030** (35.49% of 9,808), **10039** (35.45% of 8,527), and **10454** (35.28% of 9,080). These six zips cluster in the mid-30s% range but each carries hundreds to thousands of violations, making them the zips worth prioritizing.

For these higher-volume zips, `trend_data.csv` shows Class C counts climbing through 2025 to a peak around late 2025/early 2026 — e.g., 10454 peaked at 306 (2025-12), 10039 at 288 (2026-01), 10030 at 277 (2026-01), and 10009 at 192 (2025-11) — then declining across 2026, falling to 42 (10454), 66 (10039), 65 (10030), and 43 (10009) by 2026-08. So for the top zips by volume, Class C violations are trending **down** in the latter part of the covered period, after an earlier-2025-to-early-2026 rise. Note the final month (2026-08) may reflect a partial reporting period, so the most recent single data point should be read with that caveat; the multi-month decline from the late-2025/early-2026 peak through mid-2026 is visible independent of that last point.
